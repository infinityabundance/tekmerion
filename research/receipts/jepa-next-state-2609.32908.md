## Exact quotes

> Neural theorem provers must both propose tactics and decide which valid successor states to
> explore. We study whether one-step Lean transitions provide a self-supervised signal for branch
> ordering.

> A JEPA-style model predicts latent successor representations and scores only kernel-validated,
> nonterminal successors generated by a fixed pretrained ByT5 proposer. JEPA achieves higher
> Top-1 than matched InfoNCE on a same-theorem ranking diagnostic (50.18% versus 31.55%), but
> averages 282.3 of 987 solved theorems across three seeds versus 308 for proposer ordering,
> while requiring more tactic checks.

> In this setting, accurate one-step transition ranking is therefore insufficient as a
> long-horizon search value. The controlled evaluation separates representation from proposal
> quality and treats kernel-checked proof completion as the primary endpoint.

## What it establishes

- A **negative result, with a controlled design**, for the hypothesis that better one-step
  next-state prediction yields better search. It improves the ranking diagnostic
  (50.18% vs 31.55% Top-1) and still loses on the endpoint that matters (282.3 vs 308 of 987
  solved), at higher tactic-check cost.
- The paper treats kernel-checked completion as the primary endpoint, which is the correct
  endpoint for the Tekmerion programme.

## What it does not establish

- That *all* dense or residual signals fail. It tests one signal family (JEPA latent
  next-state prediction) with a fixed proposer.
- Anything about proof-term/hole residuals, which are not next-state predictions.
- Anything about object code or HOL Light.

## Why this receipt is load-bearing

It is the strongest published counterexample to the family of claims that a better
forecast/next-state residual should improve proof search. Any dense-reward or residual
mechanism (N4, M-E, and the older R3 "forecast residual") must be pre-registered against this
result, not presented as an obvious win.
